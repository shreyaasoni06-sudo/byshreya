02 -- The problem

Enshittification.

What is enshittification?

The term -- coined by writer Cory Doctorow -- describes how digital platforms degrade over time. First they are good for users. Then they abuse users to benefit business customers. Then they abuse business customers to claw back value for themselves. Then they die. It is a cycle. And mobile gaming is deep in it.

The problem with mobile gaming today is not a technical one. It is a fun one. Games that were once genuinely entertaining have slowly been rebuilt around keeping you clicking rather than keeping you engaged. The mechanic has not changed -- it has just been optimised for extraction rather than enjoyment. Players have noticed.

03 -- What comes next

Four signals pointing to how mobile gaming rebuilds trust.

Downloads have declined for four straight years. Revenue keeps climbing because players are spending more inside fewer titles they actually trust. The question is what trust-building looks like in practice.

Signal 01

Cozy gaming as aspirational proxy

Google Trends searches for "cozy gaming" are rising. 72% of UK Gen Z say homeownership matters to them but they cannot access it. Gaming is where they rehearse the aspiration. Zillow entered World of Warcraft to promote in-game homes. Brands ignoring this are missing the emotional architecture of a generation.

Signal 02

AI floods the market -- depth becomes the moat

When anyone can build a game quickly, volume explodes and differentiation collapses. The pressure is already there: downloads are down, but players who stay spend more. In a market of infinite content, the games that hold attention will be the ones that feel genuinely made rather than generated.

Signal 03

Bring back the fan letter

Game developer Panic receives thousands of physical letters monthly from players. Finish the game, send a letter, get a patch in return. Players do not just want to play -- they want to be known. Loyalty is not a points system. It is a relationship. Most platforms have engineered that out entirely.

Signal 04

Give players a stake

57% of solo players still seek social connection within games, according to Plarium research (2026). Guild systems, cooperative objectives, and creative ownership -- giving players genuine input into how a game world develops -- correlate directly with higher retention and longer playtime. Cosmetic choice is not co-creation.

04 -- The thesis

The players are still here. The fun went somewhere.

3 billion people still play mobile games. Revenue hit $81.7bn in 2026. Downloads have been falling for four years -- but players who stay are spending more inside fewer titles. They are not leaving. They are getting selective.

In a thread about everything going wrong with mobile gaming, a player spontaneously names Supercell as the company getting it right. Not because of a marketing campaign -- because the games are actually fun. That is the signal worth tracking.

The weakest signal in any category is often the one that becomes the most defensible moat. Right now, the signal is fun. The question is who is building around it before it becomes obvious.
